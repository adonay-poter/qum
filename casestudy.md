Scientific Case Studies & Use Cases: The QUM Protocol in Clinical and Behavioral Settings

This document presents five detailed, critical, and scientifically mapped case studies exploring the application of the QUM (Quick Urge Management) protocol across various behavioral and chemical addictions. Each case study critically analyzes the user's clinical profile, neurobiological mapping, wave-surfing execution, long-term systemic engagement, and the honest physiological or cognitive limitations of the platform.

Executive Summary of Core Scientific Frameworks

The QUM protocol operates at the intersection of three established neurobiological and cognitive paradigms, which form the basis of the evaluations below:

Incentive-Sensitization Theory (Robinson & Berridge, 1993, 2016): Dissociates the neural circuitry of "wanting" (mesolimbic dopaminergic pathways) from "liking" (hedonic hotspots mediated by local opioid and endocannabinoid systems). Cravings represent a sensitized dopaminergic reflex rather than a signal of projected pleasure.

Elaborated Intrusion (EI) Theory of Desire (Kavanagh, Andrade, & May, 2005): Models cravings as a dual-stage process: automatic cognitive intrusion followed by vivid, active mental elaboration. The elaboration phase relies entirely on the visuospatial sketchpad (visual RAM) of working memory. Supplying a competing visuospatial task "crashes" the mental rendering of the craving.

Extinction Learning (Bouton, 2004, 2020): Teaches the brain that a conditioned cue (trigger) can exist without the subsequent unconditioned stimulus (behavior/reward). This creates a new, inhibitory memory trace over the old association.

Case Study 1: The High-Frequency Passive Loop (Smartphone & Short-Form Video Addiction)

1. Clinical Profile & Behavioral Context

User: Maya, 26-year-old UX designer and remote worker.

Addiction Category: Compulsive Social Media/Short-Form Video Consumption ("Doomscrolling").

Triggers: Cognitive fatigue, transitional gaps in work tasks (micro-boredom), and evening dysphoria.

Baseline Behavior: Intermittent, uncontrolled pick-ups resulting in $4.5\text{ to }6$ hours of daily passive scrolling on short-video feeds. Severe prefrontal depletion, occupational procrastination, and disrupted sleep architecture.

2. Neurobiological & Psychopathological Mapping

Under the I-PACE model (Brand et al., 2019), Maya's behavior has transitioned from goal-directed gratification to an automatic, habit-based reaction. Short-form video platforms operate on a variable-ratio reinforcement schedule, delivering highly stimulating, personalized algorithmic content unpredictably.
This constant passive ingestion lowers her attentional readiness, trapping her default mode network (DMN) in an under-stimulated, passive "alerting" state (Zhai et al., 2025). When she experiences a transitional gap, her "wanting" system fires a mechanical dopamine spike. If she suppresses the urge to pick up the phone, her anxiety climbs, prompting automatic compliance to relieve the discomfort.

3. The 10-Minute Wave Sequence

Phase 1 (Stillness - 180s): Maya hits the Urge CTA. She selects the Stillness modality, initiating a multi-step sensory grounding sequence (e.g., "Identify 5 blue items in your immediate physical environment"). The system requires her to actively click "Next" (minimum 8s per step), interrupting her passive, zoned-out state.

Phase 2 (Cognitive PoW - 300s): Maya is channeled into a visuospatial cognitive task. Since she lacks personal artifacts (no voice memo or letter saved), the app routes her directly to a local, visuospatial mental rotation puzzle (e.g., "Mentally rotate this complex 3D shape and identify its top-down profile").

AI Proof-of-Work (PoW): She must sketch the rotated shape on screen or describe the perspective shifts to submit to the Edge function (validate-pow). This active, generative work forces executive control, pulling her brain out of the passive, receptive state associated with doomscrolling.

Phase 3 (Box Breathing - 64s): Complete unskippable box breathing ($4\text{s inhale} \rightarrow 4\text{s hold} \rightarrow 4\text{s exhale} \rightarrow 4\text{s hold}$ for 4 cycles) to stabilize her autonomic state before returning her to her workstation.

4. Long-Term Extinction & Systemic Engagement

Maya sets a Commitment on the home screen during high-risk hours (14:00 to 17:00) for a 3-hour block: "I will keep my phone on the far side of the room while designing." If she tries to access her phone to scroll, the commitment intercepts the gesture. Her baseline resilience score begins at 50. By completing the wave, her client-side optimistic UI and server-side DB triggers update her score to:

$$\text{Resilience} = \text{Clamp}(50 + \Delta_{\text{wave}})$$

Over 30 days, repeated exposure to the transitional micro-boredom without the subsequent scrolling behavior facilitates extinction learning, degrading her automatic reflex to reach for the device.

5. Critical Scientific Limitations & Risk Assessment

The "Device-in-Hand" Paradox: Maya must use the very instrument of her addiction (her smartphone) to run the therapeutic protocol. Tapping the QUM app places her one swipe away from the target social media apps. If her executive control is severely depleted, she faces a high risk of cross-app distraction before she can tap the "Urge" button.

Visuospatial vs. Direct Habit Loop: Passive scrolling relies heavily on physical motor habits (the thumb swipe). While the mental rotation task successfully competes for her visuospatial sketchpad, it does not satisfy or fully redirect the somatic, muscle-memory motor reflex of swiping, which may migrate to other physical objects.

Case Study 2: The Somato-Sensory Cue Trigger (Nicotine / Electronic Vaping Addiction)

1. Clinical Profile & Behavioral Context

User: Marcus, 31-year-old marketing coordinator.

Addiction Category: Compulsive Nicotine Inhalation (Vaping).

Triggers: Interpersonal stress, physical transitions (walking to his car, finishing a meal), and environmental cues (seeing his vape device on his desk).

Baseline Behavior: Vaping $1.5\text{ to }2\text{ mL}$ of high-concentration nicotine salts daily. Chronic physical restlessness, elevated resting heart rate, and severe acute anxiety when attempting cold-turkey cessation.

2. Neurobiological & Psychopathological Mapping

Nicotine is a potent agonist of nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs), causing a rapid release of dopamine in the nucleus accumbens. Over years of use, Marcus's brain has highly sensitized these pathways (Robinson & Berridge, 2008).
When a stressor hits, his sympathetic nervous system activates, causing elevated cortisol, peripheral vasoconstriction, and a racing heart. This somatic state is interpreted by his insula as a profound visceral craving. Under acute stress, his prefrontal cortex (PFC) is downregulated, leaving him highly impulsive.

[Trigger Event] ---> [Sympathetic Hyper-Arousal] ---> [PFC Downregulation] ---> [Automatic Vaping Reflex]
       |                                                                                |
       +---> (QUM Interception) ---> [Phase 1: Cold Modality] ---> [PFC Restored] ------+


3. The 10-Minute Wave Sequence

Phase 1 (Cold Modality - 180s): Marcus triggers the wave. He selects the Cold modality. The app instructs him to splash freezing water on his face for 30 seconds or hold a cold, damp cloth to his neck.

Physiological Mechanism: Cold stimulation to the ophthalmic branch of the trigeminal nerve triggers the mammalian dive reflex, causing rapid vagal activation, transient bradycardia (slowing the heart rate), and immediate downregulation of sympathetic hyper-arousal. This metabolizes the acute adrenaline spike.

Phase 2 (Hear Yourself / Personal Artifacts - t ≥ 240s): Because Marcus recorded a highly personal Voice Memo during his onboarding calibrating his "North Star" (Physical Freedom), the app displays this option. He selects "Hear yourself." The app plays his 30-second audio. The "I heard it" button is strictly time-gated to the duration of the audio, blocking premature skipping.

Early Exit Check: At $t = 250\text{s}$, the "The urge passed" link appears. Marcus taps it.

Exit Gate: The screen forces a 5-second pause to prevent impulsive clicking. Marcus rates his current urge as a $2/10$ (rating $\le 3$ is required to exit). He completes one guided, deep slow breath ($5\text{s in} \rightarrow 5\text{s out}$) and exits early as a completed wave (completion_mode: early_exit), earning +50 XP.

4. Long-Term Extinction & Systemic Engagement

Marcus engages with the Calm-Hour Ritual every Sunday morning at 10:00 (his calculated calmest hour). He reviews his 7-day pattern heatmap, listens to his voice memo, and writes a weekly reflection on what triggers got past his defenses. His calculated resilience score increases by $+5$ for completing the Calm-Hour, helping buffer his scores against any mid-week failures.

5. Scientific Limitations & Risk Assessment

Inability to Mitigate Physical Withdrawal: The QUM protocol is a cognitive-behavioral tool; it has no direct pharmacological capacity to mitigate systemic nicotine withdrawal (such as headaches, irritability, or concentration deficits caused by down-regulated nAChR density). During acute physical withdrawal peaks (24 to 72 hours post-cessation), cognitive visuospatial task-switching may feel impossibly demanding, leading to a high rate of session abandonment (rage-quitting).

Context-Specific Extinction Failure: Because extinction learning is highly context-dependent (Conklin & Tiffany, 2002), if Marcus only practices the QUM protocol in his home office, the inhibitory memory trace will fail to generalize when he is at a social venue or highly stressed at his physical workplace, resulting in sudden, unbuffered relapse.

Case Study 3: The Highly Elaborated Behavioral Craving (Compulsive Online Sports Betting & Day Trading)

1. Clinical Profile & Behavioral Context

User: Thomas, 42-year-old financial analyst.

Addiction Category: Compulsive Sports Gambling / High-Risk Options Trading.

Triggers: Seeing sports notification alerts, financial market volatility, or experiencing financial stress.

Baseline Behavior: Compulsive monitoring of live odds and high-leverage option chains, executing $10\text{ to }30$ micro-transactions a day. Severe financial volatility, marital distress, and obsessive mental rehearsal of potential "wins."

2. Neurobiological & Psychopathological Mapping

Thomas's pathology is driven by highly active mental imagery. When an opportunity arises, his mind initiates Stage 2: Elaboration under Elaborated Intrusion Theory (Kavanagh, Andrade, & May, 2005). He mentally simulates the financial windfall, the sensation of being correct, and the relief of recovering previous losses.
This complex, highly rewarding mental movie runs continuously on his visuospatial sketchpad, consuming substantial working memory and driving high-tension anticipation. This visual elaboration generates an intense psychological craving that demands behavioral execution (placing the bet).

[Market Cue] ---> [Intrusive Thought] ---> [Visual Elaboration of the "Win" on Visuospatial Sketchpad] ---> [Gambling Action]
                          |
                   (QUM Phase 2) ---> [High-Demand Spatial Puzzle] (Saturates Visual RAM) ---> [Craving Imagery Collapses]


3. The 10-Minute Wave Sequence

Phase 1 (Body Modality - 180s): Thomas taps Urge. He selects Body. Because his resilience-scaled difficulty is high, the app prescribes a demanding physical baseline challenge: 25 rapid push-ups. He completes them and taps the physical target verifier. This intense physical exertion rapidly shifts his mood state, disrupting his obsessive mental loops.

Phase 2 (Cognitive - 300s): The app routes him to "Think it through" to engage in a highly demanding spatial puzzle.

Psychological Task Alignment: The app presents a 3D block-stacking puzzle where he must mentally determine how many blocks are hidden from sight in a complex structure. This task actively competes for the exact same cognitive RAM (the visuospatial sketchpad) as his gambling fantasies.

Because his brain cannot simultaneously render the 3D block geometry and his sports betting imagery, the gambling fantasy loses resolution, "crashing" the craving. He submits his answer for verification.

Phase 3 (Box Breathing - 64s): Thomas completes the box breathing cycles, allowing his heart rate and prefrontal cortical function to return to equilibrium before the session locks and marks the wave complete.

4. Long-Term Extinction & Systemic Engagement

If Thomas encounters a severe trigger and closes the app mid-wave, the Session Lock Guard immediately flags this on next boot. The app registers a failed wave in his waves_log and activates the neutral RageQuitOverlay to reassure him without shame.
To rebuild his resilience, Thomas uses the Commitments system to pledge a 12-hour lock: "I will delete all betting applications and not check stock prices until tomorrow morning." If he breaks this, he must manually log it as broken, which drops his resilience score by $-2$, encouraging him to hold the line.

5. Scientific Limitations & Risk Assessment

The Problem of "Chasing" Dynamics: In active gambling addiction, the urge to gamble is often driven by a desperate, panicked desire to win back lost money ("loss-chasing"). This panic activates deep survival and fear-based neural networks. The visuospatial interference of Phase 2 might temporarily disrupt the visual projection of the gamble, but it does not address the underlying, panic-driven financial distress, which can easily re-trigger the urge the moment the 10-minute wave session terminates.

Cognitive Task Saturation Tolerance: If Thomas is highly accustomed to high-stress, high-stimulus environments (like live trading floors), simple mobile spatial puzzles may fail to sufficiently engage his attention, allowing him to easily run the task on "autopilot" while continuing to mentally elaborate his next bet.

Case Study 4: The Stress-Induced Escape Loop (Compulsive Nighttime Overeating)

1. Clinical Profile & Behavioral Context

User: Sarah, 35-year-old university researcher and mother of two.

Addiction Category: Compulsive Late-Night Binge Eating (highly processed, high-sugar foods).

Triggers: Physical exhaustion at $22:00$, emotional decompression after children go to sleep, and loneliness.

Baseline Behavior: Nightly, semi-dissociated consumption of highly palatable foods (chips, ice cream) in the kitchen while standing up, consuming up to $1,000$ unneeded calories in single sessions. Chronic shame, digestive distress, and weight gain.

2. Neurobiological & Psychopathological Mapping

Sarah is caught in a classic Wanting-Liking gap (Berridge & Robinson, 2016). When exhausted, her cognitive control is completely depleted. Her brain seeks a rapid hedonic rescue from the emotional and physical fatigue of the day.
The highly palatable food acts as a supernormal stimulus. However, because she eats in a rapid, dissociated state, her actual "liking" (the genuine hedonic enjoyment of the food) is highly suppressed. She consumes food quickly to satisfy a hyper-sensitized "wanting" dopamine spike, experiencing severe post-binge regret.

[Exhaustion / Solitude] ---> [Dopamine Wanting Spike] ---> [Rapid Dissociated Ingestion] ---> [Severe Post-Binge Regret]
          |                                                                                      ^
   (QUM Interception) ---> [Phase 2: Read Personal Letter] ---> [Re-establish Agency] -----------+


3. The 10-Minute Wave Sequence

Phase 1 (Stillness - 180s): Sarah feels the urge to head to the pantry. She sits on her living room couch and taps Urge. She selects Stillness, launching an observation exercise: "Close your eyes and identify the three loudest sounds in your environment, then focus on the physical sensation of your back against the couch cushions." This stops her automatic movement toward the kitchen and establishes physical stillness.

Phase 2 (Read What You Wrote Yourself - t ≥ 8s): Sarah has an active, deeply personal Letter to Future You stored in her local appStorage and synced to the Supabase DB. She selects this option.

The Personal Artifact: The app displays her letter, written during her last Calm-Hour ritual: "Sarah, when you are standing in the dark kitchen at 22:30, you aren't actually hungry. You are just exhausted and lonely. You deserve real rest, not a stomach ache. Take a deep breath and go to bed."

The Read Gate: The "I read it" CTA is disabled for 8 seconds, forcing her to look at her own words. This self-generated, compassionate insight restores her sense of personal agency and self-efficacy.

Victory Screen: The wave concludes successfully. She is greeted with her current reward stats ($+50\text{ XP}$, $+5\text{ Resilience}$), and her letter remains displayed alongside her progress. She closes the app and goes directly to bed.

4. Long-Term Extinction & Systemic Engagement

To support her progress, the local notification system utilizes the Predictive Urge Warning engine. Based on Sarah's historical reflection logs, the system recognizes her peak vulnerability window (22:00 to 23:00) and schedules a local notification at 21:45 with a grounding insight: "The house is quiet. It's almost 22:00. This is your peak window. Keep your phone close and be ready to surf." This proactive alert prepares her prefrontal defense systems before the urge strikes.

5. Critical Scientific Limitations & Risk Assessment

The Exhaustion Exhaust-Valve: When a user is in a state of extreme physiological sleep deprivation, the neurological capacity to initiate any self-regulatory behavior is profoundly compromised. Sarah may simply lack the cognitive stamina to open the app, choosing instead to bypass the system entirely.

Satiety Center Delays: Behavioral urge-surfing can successfully manage the anticipated reward loop. However, if Sarah is experiencing genuine, physiological leptin/ghrelin dysregulation (due to poor dietary composition or sleep deficit), the biological driver of hunger will persist past the 10-minute wave, rendering behavioral extinction highly unstable without concurrent nutritional intervention.

Case Study 5: The Habitual Visual Loop (Compulsive Pornography & Adult Media Use)

1. Clinical Profile & Behavioral Context

User: Daniel, 22-year-old university student.

Addiction Category: Compulsive Use of Online Adult Media (Pornography).

Triggers: Academic boredom, study-related anxiety, visual triggers on mainstream social media, and being alone in his dormitory room.

Baseline Behavior: Daily compulsive viewing sessions lasting $1\text{ to }3$ hours, typically late at night. Severe escalation in material intensity, occupational dysfunction, and profound moral and personal distress.

2. Neurobiological & Psychopathological Mapping

Compulsive pornographic use represents a powerful convergence of visual cue-reactivity and rapid, potent dopaminergic reinforcement. Daniel's triggers immediately activate highly vivid, visual reward-anticipation imagery on his visuospatial sketchpad (Elaborated Intrusion Theory).
Because adult media consumption is highly visual, the intrusive thoughts rapidly expand into a detailed mental rehearsal of the search and selection process. If he tries to white-knuckle his way through the urge, the extinction burst phenomenon (Lattal et al.) occurs: his brain, sensing a loss of access to the high-reward stimulus, temporarily spikes the craving's intensity to force compliance, leading to willpower exhaustion.

[Boredom / Isolation] ---> [Visual Prompt] ---> [Visuospatial Elaboration] ---> [Willpower Exhaustion] ---> [Compulsive Action]
          |                                                                                  ^
   (QUM Interception) ---> [Phase 2: Competing Visual Task] ---> [Extinction Burst Managed] -+


3. The 10-Minute Wave Sequence

Phase 1 (Body Modality - 180s): Alone in his room, Daniel feels the urge and opens QUM. He selects Body. Because his resilience tier is low, the app prescribes a moderate physical baseline challenge: 15 bodyweight squats. This forces him to stand up, step away from his desk, change his physical orientation, and metabolizes the immediate somatic restlessness.

Phase 2 (Cognitive - 300s): Daniel enters the cognitive phase. Because he is highly vulnerable to visual cues, the app deploys an Ethiopian-context visuospatial task to engage his spatial memory: "Close your eyes and mentally trace the shape of a traditional Mesob (woven basket). Now, describe the alternating color patterns of the weave in detail."

The Grounding Mechanism: This highly specific, non-judgmental, culturally-grounded spatial reconstruction task fully occupies his visual RAM. It forces him to construct a complex geometric shape in his mind, completely overwriting and "starving" the highly-arousing pornographic imagery that was previously dominating his attention. He types his description and submits it for validation.

Phase 3 (Box Breathing - 64s): The app locks him into 4 cycles of box breathing. The slow, structured pacing lowers his heart rate, stabilizes his blood pressure, and allows his nervous system to transition back into a relaxed parasympathetic state.

4. Long-Term Extinction & Systemic Engagement

Daniel sets a Commitment on his dashboard: "I will keep my computer closed after 23:00 tonight." If he successfully maintains this, the system rewards him with positive reinforcement on his home screen.
His weekly Calm-Hour Ritual allows him to objectively analyze his triggers. Over months of consistent wave completions, his brain undergoes neuroplastic reorganization: the conditioned link between the visual prompts and the immediate compulsive behavior degrades, steadily shrinking the baseline intensity of his cravings.

5. Critical Scientific Limitations & Risk Assessment

Extinction Burst Relapse Risk: During the first 14 days of using QUM, Daniel is highly likely to experience a massive extinction burst. As his brain realizes he is consistently refusing to engage in the compulsive behavior, his internal wanting circuits will generate exceptionally intense, panic-driven cravings. If he does not have secondary environmental barriers in place (such as system-level web blockers), the cognitive load of the spatial task may be completely overwhelmed by the sheer intensity of the extinction burst, leading to a rage-quit.

Privacy and Safe Spaces: Because Phase 1 requires physical movement (squats, cold water) and Phase 2 may require vocalizing or sketching, Daniel must be in a private, safe space to execute the protocol. If he experiences an urge while in a quiet university library or public computer lab, the physical and audio components of QUM are socially unviable, leaving him without a functional intervention unless he can safely retreat to his dormitory.
